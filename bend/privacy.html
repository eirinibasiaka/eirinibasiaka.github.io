Privacy Policy — Bend

Last updated 23 September 2026

Bend is a tile-link puzzle game for iPhone, published by Eirini Basiaka. This page says what the app does with information, in the same words the app itself uses. If anything here is unclear, write to eirinibasiaka.dev@gmail.com.

There is no account

Bend has no sign-in, no profile and no server of ours. Nothing you do in the game is sent to us, and we do not run analytics of our own. We never see your name, your email address or your device.

Your progress stays on your phone

The level you have reached, the board you left, your sound and haptics switches and whether you have bought Remove ads are written to the app's own storage on the device. Deleting the app deletes them. They are not backed up to us and they are not shared.

Advertising

Bend shows one kind of ad and one only: an optional rewarded video, offered at a single moment — when a board has no matching pair left and you may take a shuffle. You can always decline it, and there is always a way on without it. There are no banners, no interstitials, no app-open ads and no offerwalls.

Those videos come from Google AdMob (the Google Mobile Ads SDK). When an ad is requested or watched, Google may collect and use information from your device — device and advertising identifiers, usage data and diagnostics — for advertising, for measuring ads and for detecting fraud. That processing is Google's, under Google's own terms:

  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • AdMob's own description of the data it collects

The app declares its own data use in the privacy manifest shipped inside it (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy): Bend's own code collects nothing and tracks nothing. The Google Mobile Ads SDK ships its own manifest beside it, and the App Store listing's privacy labels follow it.

Consent, and tracking

Nothing is asked at first launch. After you have finished the first level and its card has gone, Bend asks in this order:

  • A consent message, where the law where you are requires one (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland). It is shown through Google's User Messaging Platform and it decides whether ads may be requested at all, and whether they may be personalised. If you refuse, the ad SDK is not started in that session.
  • The iOS tracking permission (App Tracking Transparency), asked once. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, no advertising identifier is shared for tracking across other companies' apps and websites, and any ad you are shown is non-personalised.

You are given nothing in the game for answering either one in any particular way. If you have bought Remove ads, neither question is asked at all, because there is no ad for you to consent to.

How to change your mind

  • In Bend, tap the gear, then Ad privacy options to reopen the consent message and change your answer. That pill appears wherever there is a consent message to reopen.
  • For tracking, use Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking on your iPhone.
  • Privacy policy, in the same place in the app, opens this page.

Purchases

Bend sells one thing: Remove ads, a single non-consumable purchase that makes the one rewarded shuffle of each attempt come without a video. It is bought and restored through Apple's App Store. We never see or store a card number; the transaction is Apple's, and Apple's privacy policy covers it.

Children

Bend is rated 12+ and is not part of the App Store's Kids Category. It is not directed at children under the age of digital consent, and it is not knowingly used to collect information from them. If you believe a child has provided information through the app, write to the address below and it will be dealt with.

Changes to this policy

If what the app does changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. The previous wording stays in the app's repository history.
