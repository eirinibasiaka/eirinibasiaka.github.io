Support — Bend

Last updated 23 September 2026

Bend is a tile-link puzzle for iPhone, made by Eirini Basiaka. If your question is not answered here, write to eirinibasiaka.dev@gmail.com and say which iPhone and which version of iOS you are on. It is one person reading, so a reply takes a day or two.

How the game works

Tap two tiles of the same kind. They clear if a path of at most three straight segments joins them through empty cells. The path is allowed to leave the board and come back around the outside, so two tiles on opposite edges are often connectable when they do not look it.

After every pair the remaining tiles fall. From level 8 the direction the tiles fall keeps turning — down, left, up, right — every third match at first, more often as the levels go on. The compass above the board shows the current direction, and the board's frame glows on the side the tiles fall toward. One match before it turns, that edge pulses and the compass wobbles toward where it is going.

There is no timer. Three hints per level are free.

The board has no moves left

That is a real state of the game, not a bug, and it is reachable by playing badly rather than by being given an impossible board: every level is solved by a program before it ships, so each board you are given can be cleared.

When it happens, a card slides up:

  • The first shuffle of every attempt is free. No video, no question.
  • After that, exactly one more shuffle is offered, as an optional video. You can always decline it. If no video is ready, the shuffle is given anyway and the same one offer is spent.
  • After that, the card offers Restart level and nothing else. There is nothing to buy to keep playing.

Restarting is a new attempt, so both shuffles come back.

Ads

There is one kind of ad in Bend and one only: the optional rewarded video above. There are no banners, no interstitials, no app-open ads and no offerwalls. The video is never required to finish a level.

Remove ads, and how to restore it

Remove ads is a single purchase that makes that one rewarded shuffle come without a video. It does not buy extra shuffles and it does not change a board.

If you have bought it before — on this phone or another one signed in to the same Apple Account — get it back either way:

  • In Bend, tap the gear, then Restore purchases. It finishes in a few seconds and the ad offer stops appearing.
  • If that does not find it, check you are signed in to the Apple Account that made the purchase: Settings › [your name] › Media & Purchases. Then try again.

Purchases are Apple's. Refunds are Apple's too: use reportaproblem.apple.com. We cannot issue one, and we never see your payment details.

Changing your ad privacy choices

  • In Bend, tap the gear, then Ad privacy options. That reopens the consent message and lets you change your answer. The pill appears wherever there is a consent message to reopen — where you are, the law may not require one.
  • For tracking, use Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking on your iPhone.
  • Privacy policy, in the same place in the app, opens the privacy page.

Your progress

The level you have reached, the board you left in the middle, your sound and haptics switches and whether you have bought Remove ads are written to the app's own storage on the phone. There is no account and no cloud save, so deleting the app deletes your progress, and it does not move to a new phone on its own. Remove ads does come back, through Restore purchases.

Sound and haptics

Both have their own switch behind the gear and both remember what you set. If the game is silent when it should not be, check the switch first, then the iPhone's ring/silent switch and its volume.

Accessibility

Every tile kind is a shape as well as a colour, so the board can be told apart without colour vision. If something is unreadable or unreachable for you, that is worth an email — say what you use and what happened.

Something is wrong

Write to eirinibasiaka.dev@gmail.com with:

  • the iPhone model and the iOS version;
  • the level number, if it is a particular board;
  • what you did, what happened, and what you expected instead.

A board you believe cannot be cleared is worth reporting with its level number: every board is proved solvable before it ships, so a real counter-example would be a bug worth fixing.
